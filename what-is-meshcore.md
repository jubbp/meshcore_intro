## What Is MeshCore?

MeshCore is a way to pass messages from companion (personal) devices  and home based small repeaters into a local system so people can communicate even when normal phone or internet services are unreliable.

In practical terms, it can help people:

- stay in touch during outages
- share short updates with people nearby
- build a community network that does not depend on one central tower

## The three node roles

MeshCore networks in this region can be easier to understand if you think about three kinds of nodes:

- Companion nodes: the devices people use day to day
- Local repeater nodes: nodes that help messages reach further across a town or local area
- District repeater nodes: higher-level repeaters that help carry traffic between larger areas

You do not need to know the technical details to use the network. The useful idea is simple: some nodes are for everyday use, and some nodes are there to help messages travel further.

## A simple way to think about it

Imagine people in two small groups, each with a helper in the middle, and one bigger helper above them both. The everyday devices talk to their nearby helper, and the bigger helper helps join the groups together.

That is the basic idea behind a mesh network.

## Why people use it

- Emergency preparedness and local resilience
- Neighborhood coordination
- Community events and volunteer groups
- Learning and experimentation with local networking

## What MeshCore is good for

MeshCore is most useful when the goal is simple local communication.

For example, people may use it to:

- let nearby volunteers share updates during an event
- coordinate around power, weather, or outage situations
- check in with a group when mobile coverage is poor or busy
- learn how local networks work in a practical, hands-on way

## What users notice day to day

People do not need to think about the technical side to get value from it.

What they usually notice is:

- they can send short messages to people nearby
- the network may keep working even if one path has a problem
- messages can travel further because repeater nodes help carry them
- it can be easier to share updates within a local area than to rely on a distant service

## What it is not

MeshCore is not a replacement for every phone call, text message, or internet service.

It is better thought of as an extra local communication option, especially useful when the normal options are not enough.

## Why it matters

If you only care about the result, the short version is this: MeshCore helps people keep talking when they need a local backup path for communication.

That can be valuable for families, community groups, and emergency planning.
